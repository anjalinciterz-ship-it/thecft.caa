In [Part 1](/blog/you-dont-have-to-have-everything-figured-out) we sat with a difficult admission: that you don't have to have everything figured out, and that feeling lost is not the same as failing.

This week I want to talk about what most of us do instead of sitting with it.

We speed up.

We fill the calendar. We answer every message within the hour. We take on the extra project. We scroll until our thumbs ache. We research, plan, analyse, optimise. We keep ourselves occupied, because being busy is so much easier than being still.

But what if the thing you need right now isn't another answer?

**You are allowed to slow down.**

## Movement is not the same as progress

There is nothing wrong with ambition. Wanting to grow, build, earn, improve, none of that is a spiritual problem.

But constant motion does not automatically mean you are going somewhere. Sometimes we are running simply because we are afraid of what happens when we stop.

Because when we do stop, things surface.

Thoughts we have been outrunning. Feelings we were too scheduled to feel. The quiet recognition that something in our life stopped working a while ago and we have been carrying it anyway. Occasionally that small clear voice that says: *this isn't what I need.*

That is uncomfortable. But discomfort is not always a threat to escape from. Very often it is information, arriving in the only way it can.

## Rest is not quitting

Somewhere along the way, rest got reframed as weakness. If you are not producing, responding, learning or improving, you feel like you are wasting the day. Even our rest has to be productive now. Even our stillness has to be optimised.

A tree does not grow by forcing itself upward every day of the year. There are seasons of visible growth and seasons of invisible preparation, and the second kind is not the tree being lazy. It is the tree doing the part nobody photographs.

People have seasons too.

There is a time to act and a time to reflect. A time to build and a time to heal. And there are stretches where the most meaningful thing available to you is to breathe and let something settle.

Slowing down does not mean you have given up on your life. Sometimes it means you have finally started paying attention to it.

## You cannot hear yourself over that much noise

Consider what reaches you in an average day.

Notifications. Group chats. News. Videos. Opinions. Advertisements. Other people's achievements, other people's crises, other people's expectations of you.

All of it carries an instruction, even when it doesn't sound like one. This is what you should want. This is who you should be. This is what you should be doing by now.

After enough of that, your own thinking gets very hard to locate. Not gone, just buried under everyone else's.

Which is why deliberate quiet is not a luxury. Put the phone in another room. Walk without headphones. Sit somewhere and let it be boring. Write down what you are feeling without editing it into something more presentable. Take a few slow breaths.

You do not have to turn this into a complicated practice. Start with simply being present and see what shows up.

## What your exhaustion is actually saying

Exhaustion is not always about sleep. You can be fully rested and still be tired to your bones.

Tired of pretending everything is fine.
Tired of managing everyone's feelings.
Tired of proving yourself to people who were never going to be convinced.
Tired of being the strong one by default.

When that kind of tiredness turns up, the reflex is to criticise yourself for it. Try not to. Pause instead, and ask what it is pointing at.

Maybe you need a boundary. Maybe you need help and have not asked. Maybe you need to put something down that you have been carrying out of loyalty or habit. Maybe you need to forgive yourself for something old. Maybe you just need permission to rest, and nobody is going to hand it to you, so you will have to give it to yourself.

## Sometimes the answer doesn't come because you are searching too loudly

When we want clarity, we hunt for it. We ask everyone we know. We read everything. We replay old conversations looking for the missed clue. We simulate every possible future in our heads at 1am.

At some point, searching becomes another form of noise.

Some answers are not reached by thinking harder. They are noticed. And you cannot notice anything while the volume is that high.

There are moments that ask you to stop asking and start listening.

## Ten minutes, this week

Here is the practice. It is deliberately small, because small things get done.

1. **Choose ten minutes.** Early morning or last thing at night works best, whichever has less traffic.
2. **Remove the inputs.** No phone, no music, no podcast, no tidying while you think. Sit down properly.
3. **Take three slow breaths.** Longer out than in. Let your shoulders drop on the third one.
4. **Ask: what am I feeling right now?** Whatever answers, do not argue with it. You are not required to justify a feeling.
5. **Then ask: what do I need right now?** Again, no judgment. The honest answer is often smaller and more ordinary than you expect.
6. **Write one line about it.** Not a journal entry. One line.

That's it. You are not solving your life in ten minutes. You are practising listening, and listening is where reconnection usually starts.

Do it three times this week and you will notice something: the answers get clearer each time, not because you thought harder, but because you finally left room for them.

## Don't rush your own transformation

Growth takes time. Healing takes time. Learning to be at ease with who you are takes time, and it does not move faster because you are impatient with it.

You do not have to extract a lesson from every hard week by Sunday. Some seasons are not asking you to break through. They are asking you to slow down enough to understand what they are teaching.

So if you have been overwhelmed, disconnected or quietly exhausted, consider this your permission to pause.

One breath. Then another. Put down what does not need carrying today. Let the noise drop.

Because moving forward does not always begin with another step.

Sometimes it begins with **standing still long enough to know where you are standing.**
